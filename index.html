Sheet 02 / 05 · MIT Motorsports

Rear Uprights

MIT Motorsports, MY25 car

Rear Uprights Lead, May 2024 to May 2025

The upright ties the A-arms, tie rod, brakes, gearbox and in-hub motor into a single load path, so it has to be stiff, strong and light at once. I led the rear upright for a four-wheel-drive car with in-hub motors, taking it from requirements through hand calcs, FEM and machined hardware.

Requirements and results

Targets came from test data and last year’s car.

MetricTargetAchieved
Mass per upright 725 g 607 g16% under target; 27% lighter than MY24’s 835 g
Stress Factor of safety ≥ 1.5 on yield 1.5Meets target
First mode > 270 Hz, 2× the 135 Hz driving frequency 264 Hz1.96× driving frequency, within 2.2% of target

How the design came together

Figures below are keyed to these steps

  1. 1

    Put a price on mass

    Bolted 11.6 kg of cast-iron plates to the MY24 uprights and ran 8 timed autocross laps with and without them, moving the plates to the cockpit to hold car mass constant. The gap was statistically significant: about 2 autocross points per kg, which bounded how hard to push mass reduction.

  2. 2

    Size it by hand

    From loads built on the loads team’s tire-ellipse accelerations, sized the lugs in Excel for net-section tension, shear tear-out and bearing, checked brake and tie-rod tab bending with beam theory, and sized the ribbed supports.

  3. 3

    Verify in FEM

    Modeled the upright in Siemens NX FEM with its interfacing parts, so their stiffness and mass counted, and constrained each joint to its stiffness and degrees of freedom. Glue constraints, RBE3s and CBUSHs joined the camber clevis, carrier, motor and brake caliper at the connection points. Structural runs confirmed a 1.5 minimum factor of safety; modal put the first mode at 264 Hz.

  4. 4

    Design for Manufacturing

    Kept the geometry 3-axis machinable in 7075 aluminum and dropped non-essential chamfers and fillets. Sharing geometry front to rear cut design time, cost and risk. Traded manufacturing methods early and consulted machine shop managers in design reviews.

Dual-impeller Turbopump

MIT 16.811, team of 3

Casing, mechanical elements and rotordynamics
September to December 2025

A single-shaft pump with two printed impellers and a 1 HP motor. I owned the casing architecture and mechanical elements, first-order rotordynamics and desktop tests; my teammates owned the impellers and CFD.

Rotordynamics were hand-calculated, verified in ARMD, and tested on a simplified rig with laser displacement sensors.

Casing and impellers had to be additively manufactured.

What I would change

From teardown and retest

  1. 1

    Seal service. Repeated lip-seal removal cracked the resin casing; the seal interface has to survive teardown.

  2. 2

    Bolt pattern. A uniform bolt circle with overlapping 45° preload cones, for even clamp load on the seal.

  3. 3

    Tip clearance. Finish-machine the impeller or go CNC: a separately made CNC Al 6061 impeller C reached 13.5 psi at 40 LPM, vs. 8.5 psi printed.

Sheet 04 / 05 · MIT Rocket Team

Prometheus Solid Motors

MIT Rocket Team

Solid Propulsion Lead (Spring 2024), Solid Propulsion Team Member (Fall 2023)
August 2023 to May 2024

Propulsion for Prometheus, a custom two-stage rocket for the Spaceport America Cup, verified against the competition rules by hand calc, FEA, hydrostatic test and static fire.

1 Design

Motors I helped design, test and manufacture for the Prometheus project as both a member and team lead. CAD in SOLIDWORKS.

Initial grain geometries. Booster: Bates, 4-fin finocyl, 8-fin finocyl. Sustainer: Bates.

OpenMotor thrust-curve simulations (Fig. 4.2).

Nozzle sizing and optimization. Geometry generation: the initial geometry was chosen to be a Bézier curve, plotted with a Python script; MATLAB was then used to regress the implicit function in terms of x for SOLIDWORKS.

Expansion ratio optimization: to avoid under- and over-expansion for as much of the flight profile as possible, the nozzle was optimized for 2/3 of motor burnout height. Flow in the nozzle was assumed isentropic; atmospheric pressure was calculated from height, exit Mach number from chamber pressure, and from that the exit-to-throat area ratio.

(7) Me = √2γ − 1((pcpa)(γ−1)/γ − 1)
(8) AeAt = 1Me(1 + γ−12M2eγ+12)(γ+1)/(2(γ−1))

Nozzle assembly. 4-part assembly: aluminum carrier, upper phenolic insulator, lower phenolic insulator, graphite throat.

Aluminum carrier: double O-ring seal, 24 bolts in 2 staggered rows, exterior lip for outer-case interfacing.

Phenolic insulators: canvas phenolic resin composite as ablative heat protection.

Graphite throat: high compressive strength to withstand maximum pressure at the throat.

Second-stage ignition: head-end ignition (HEI). New simple, reloadable, lightweight head-end ignition design: a 1/8 in NPT steel bolt epoxied to a 2.5 in aluminum pipe and packed with propellant scraps. The bolt has 3 step-down holes to minimize the risk of the epoxy shearing (a failed seal) without spending machining time on a fully tapered edge.

2 Manufacturing

All parts were manufactured on ProtoTrak lathes and mill. Drawings were created in SOLIDWORKS.
